# Lessons from existing body and face measurement systems

Official sources checked 11 September 2026. Read-only research; no accounts, credentials, paid services, trial registrations or private-media uploads.

| System | What establishes scale / capture requirements | Integration and evidence | What transfers to this pet tool |
|---|---|---|---|
| [3DLOOK Mobile Tailor](https://3dlook.ai/mobile-tailor/for-made-to-measure/) | Gender, **height and weight**, then guided front and side photos. Its own capture flow controls camera angle using the gyroscope; it explicitly declines arbitrary existing-photo uploads. | Hosted measurement widget, API in some plans; proprietary human models. The page makes accuracy claims but provides no reproducible per-measurement error distribution or pet validation. | Guide capture at body height, require the intended view and give immediate positioning feedback. Human height must not be silently replaced by a breed average. |
| [Bodygram Platform API](https://docs.bodygram.com/platform/first-scan) / [Headless SDK](https://docs.bodygram.com/headless) | The photo request includes **height in mm, weight in grams, age and gender**, plus front/right photos. It also offers a distinct stats-only estimation mode. | REST API and JavaScript SDK. Raw-measurement integration uses a server-held secret and short-lived browser scan token. Public examples are human-only and do not establish pet measurement accuracy. | Keep scan evidence, measurement estimation and recommendations separate. Maintain explicit success/failure states; never turn a failed photo scan into unstated default dimensions. |
| [Fittingbox PD measurement](https://fittingbox.com/en/optical-fit/online-pd-measurement-tool) | A guided **reference-card protocol** provides scale for pupil-distance estimation. Browser-based, no app download. | Commercial embeddable widget. Vendor reports error within 1 mm in 70% and within 2 mm in 96% of measurements; the page does not supply the test population/protocol. These are vendor claims for human PD, not dog body accuracy. | A visible known-size object is an established practical method. Automatically detect it, verify completeness and tilt, and explain why it is required before capture. |
| [Apple ARFaceAnchor](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arfaceanchor) | A live native ARKit face session provides facial coordinates in **meters**, eye transforms and a model fitted to the detected face. This differs from an uncalibrated uploaded JPEG. | Native Apple SDK; face geometry and tracking are available. The cited API specifies coordinate units, not a guaranteed PD measurement error or a pet-body API. | For a later native depth-capture version, preserve actual camera calibration/geometry and capability checks. Do not treat a face mesh or units named “meters” as demonstrated sizing accuracy. |

These are proprietary human measurement products or a native face framework, not reusable pretrained pet measurement engines. Their public pages do not grant a general right to modify/retrain proprietary models for animal anatomy, and none of the reviewed sources claims pet support. We can implement our own capture and validation workflow inspired by their published behavior; commercial SDK reuse or model adaptation would require the vendor's supported scope and terms. No such integration was attempted.

## Two immediate improvements

1. **Make capture readiness visible before accepting a measuring view.** Show “whole pet visible,” “side/front position detected,” and “10 cm reference detected and level” from actual detection results. After upload, automatically analyze and retain the accepted view, then request only its missing counterpart. A photo lacking scale should display the detected outline plus a specific reference request, never an empty manual form labeled as a successful scan. This follows the guided-capture pattern used by 3DLOOK and Bodygram.
2. **Use concrete quality failures and consistency checks.** [Bodygram's documented failures](https://docs.bodygram.com/platform/error-codes) distinguish blur, exposure, framing, pose and multiple subjects. Our geometry now rejects clipping, broken silhouettes, outside-mask landmarks and disagreement greater than 15% across accepted frames. The next inexpensive addition is local blur/exposure diagnostics on each decoded frame; reject severely degraded frames before estimating. Thresholds must be described as capture heuristics and tested, not advertised as measurement confidence.

Independent verification remains necessary: compare repeated scans against tape measurements on many dogs/cats, record chest/neck/back errors separately, include fur and difficult poses, and keep held-out animals. The current ellipse estimator has synthetic numerical tests and real landmark/mask trials; it does not inherit these vendors' trained human-body models or accuracy claims.
